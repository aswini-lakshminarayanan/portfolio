The one habit that changed how I ship

ProductLessons

Early in my career I measured progress by how busy I felt. More tabs open, more meetings, more Slack — surely that meant more work getting done. It didn't.

The shift

The habit that changed everything was deceptively small: write down the one outcome that matters before touching anything else.

Not a task list. One sentence describing what "done" looks like. Everything I do that day either serves that sentence or waits.

  1. It kills busywork disguised as progress.
  2. It makes trade-offs obvious.
  3. It gives the team a shared definition of success.

Why it works

Most delivery problems aren't capacity problems — they're clarity problems. When the outcome is fuzzy, effort scatters. When it's sharp, effort compounds.

Try it for a week. Write the sentence each morning. You'll be surprised how often the honest version reveals you were about to spend a day on the wrong thing.

This is the kind of note I'll share here regularly — replace it with your own.
