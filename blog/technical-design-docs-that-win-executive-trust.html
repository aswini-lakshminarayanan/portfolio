Technical design docs that win executive trust

Solution ConsultingAEM

Presenting a technical design to a C-level audience is a different job from writing one for engineers. The architecture can be flawless and still fail the room if the people funding it can't see themselves in it.

After a lot of these sessions, here's the structure I keep coming back to.

1. Open with the business outcome, not the stack

Executives don't buy AEM; they buy faster time-to-market, lower content operations cost, or a consistent brand across markets. Name that outcome in the first slide, then let the architecture follow as the means, not the headline.

2. Show one diagram, not ten

A single system diagram that a non-technical stakeholder can follow beats a stack of detailed views. Keep the detailed views in an appendix for the engineers who'll actually build it.

3. Make the trade-offs explicit

Every serious design has trade-offs: headless vs. traditional authoring, build vs. buy, phased vs. big-bang. Naming them yourself signals that you've done the thinking, and it turns the room from skeptics into collaborators.

4. Close with a path, not a wall

End on a phased plan: what ships first, what proves the risky assumption early, and what "done" looks like for phase one. A path people can start on Monday earns more trust than a perfect end-state they can't picture reaching.

None of this replaces solid engineering. But the design that gets funded and built is usually the one that respected both the architecture and the audience.
